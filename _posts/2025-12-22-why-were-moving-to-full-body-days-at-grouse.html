If you’ve been around Grouse for a while, you’ll know our training has always been structured and predictable. A clear upper/lower split that let people walk in and know exactly what they were working that day.

Over time you start to see the cracks in even the good systems. What looks organised on paper doesn’t always hold up in real bodies, week after week. People would hit big numbers on Monday, get wrecked on Tuesday, then drag themselves through Wednesday like they were surviving a small war. It worked until it didn’t.

The truth is, you don’t live your life in body-part splits.

You carry shopping bags, bend down for the kids, sprint for a tram, shift boxes, push, pull, hinge and squat, usually all in the same day. So why train like the body is a set of separate departments?

So we’re moving to full-body days, each built around a different intent instead of a different muscle group: Load, Volume, Foundation, Power.

You’ll still get structure, and you’ll still know what you’re walking into.

It stops you hammering the same pattern until something gives, and swaps it for a rhythm that moves through the week: heavy, then repeatable, then restorative, then explosive.

It also means you can show up four days in a row without feeling like you’ve missed a session.

A gym is only as good as its programming. The music and the vibe and the personalities are real, and we do deliver on those too. Underneath all of it, though, this is a space to do the work, and I’ll keep iterating on the program until it feels right for real people.

So this is the next chapter for Grouse: full-body days, with intent behind each one. Simple and structured, same as before.
