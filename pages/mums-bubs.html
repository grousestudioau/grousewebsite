What a session is like

Bring the bub and do the work.

They stay with you

Your bub comes along and hangs out on a mat in the room with you while you train. If they need you mid-session, settle them and step back in when you’re ready.

A coach runs it

A coach takes the whole session and scales every exercise to where you’re at. Groups stay capped at 8, so they can keep an eye on how everyone’s moving.

Done under an hour

The training runs about 45 minutes. We hold a full hour so there’s room for a feed or a nappy change without anyone rushing the session.

Who it’s for

Made for after birth.

  • 01

    Postpartum mums

    Every session is built for recovery after having a baby, so it’s postpartum only. If you’re currently pregnant, this isn’t the right fit for you right now.

  • 02

    Any stage, any fitness level

    First baby or fourth, you’re welcome, and you don’t need to have trained before. The weight and the difficulty are yours to set.

  • 03

    Built around your recovery

    We ease into deep core and pelvic floor work, and modify around ab separation and c-section or vaginal birth recovery. Have your postnatal check first and tell the coach anything to work around.

Do I have to bring my bub?

Bring them or come solo, whatever works that week. If you do bring them, pack a mat for them to lie on.

What if my bub needs me mid-session?

Feed or change them, then step back in when you’re ready.

How old can my bub be?

Bring them from newborn right up until they start walking. Once they’re on their feet it’s time to graduate them out, since the studio sits close to the road and that keeps everyone safe.

I’ve never trained before, is that okay?

Yes. A coach runs the whole session and scales every exercise to your level, so you can walk in knowing nothing.

What does it cost?

Your first session is free. After that it’s $35 a casual session, or book in with your ClassPass credits.

Anything to sort before my first one?

If you’re returning after birth, have your postnatal check and let the coach know about a c-section, ab separation, or anything else to work around.
