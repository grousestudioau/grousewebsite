Why lift

Strength matters more with age.

Muscle and bone

Australia's guidelines for people over 65 recommend strength exercises two or three times a week to help keep muscle and bone strong.

Balance and falls

The World Health Organization recommends people over 65 train balance and strength on three or more days a week to help prevent falls.

The rest of your life

Carrying the shopping, climbing stairs, getting up off the floor. We train the movements behind them: squats, hinges, presses, pulls and carries.

Sources: Australian Government Department of Health, WHO guidelines on physical activity (2020).

How we set you up

Same session, your level.

  • 01

    Every exercise scales

    A coach runs every session and adjusts each exercise to you. If a full squat doesn't suit your knees, you'll squat to a box. You set the weight.

  • 02

    Small groups

    We cap groups at 12, so your coach can see how you're moving and step in when something needs changing.

  • 03

    Start light, build up

    Haven't trained in years? Start at a level that feels easy and add a little over time. The health guidelines give the same advice.

  • 04

    Tell us what to work around

    A joint replacement, arthritis, a dodgy shoulder. Tell your coach before your first session. If you have a heart condition or you're not sure, check with your GP first.
